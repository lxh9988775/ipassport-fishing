Five waters now, and where you stand finally matters: two spots per water, each with its own regulars.

1. Added the estuary and the night pier — mullet on the mudflats, black bream and eels in the tidal channel, catfish under the lights and mandarin fish around the pilings. That brings the guide to 27 species. Old favourites such as bass and grouper also turn up in the new waters without changing their place in the guide.
2. Ten spots each carry a short hint about what tends to bite. A spot only raises the odds of its featured fish; nothing is locked away, and there are no timers, gates or penalties. Moving spots just makes rare visitors a little less rare.
3. The guide is now a list plus detail view. Rows show water and spot, time and bait, and length; the detail page adds the spot of your first catch to your history. Old saves migrate automatically and keep every catch.
4. The main screen uses a two-line badge for water and spot, with the daily fish news still visible alongside it. The cover now reads 27 species, five waters and ten spots.
5. Fixed an issue where the first screen could go blank or freeze on startup on some devices; launching the game is now more stable.
6. Reeling in is no longer a constant sprint: fish now move in a cycle of cruise, wind-up warning, dash and rest, and pause and turn red before dashing. Small fish such as the whitebait mostly cruise slowly, while rare species change pace often. The catch zone is about 30% smaller; press Down for a fast rod to chase dashes and Up for a slow rod for fine control. Also fixed the progress bar barely rising even with the fish inside the zone — it now climbs steadily while you hold the fish, and only slips back slowly when you miss.
7. Added a fourth rod, the Heavy Rod: the largest catch zone and enough speed to follow a dash, built for hard-charging fish such as the yellow pike and sailfish, at a slightly lower score multiplier. High-score runs still favour the lure rod. Also fixed big fish dashing faster than any rod could physically follow.
