This version retunes the reeling feel and difficulty: easier to land fish, more responsive controls, smoother visuals. Fish, rods, spots, levels and the codex are unchanged, and your codex progress and high score carry over.

1. Easier to land fish: fish swim and dash more slowly, the catch bar (the green band) is larger, and you start with more progress on the bar. Before, even common fish could be impossible on an unlucky run; now common fish are reliable and rare ones are within reach with a little practice.
2. Wider strike window: the deep sea used to give you only 0.8 seconds to strike, which is tight one-handed on a small screen. All three spots are more forgiving now, so striking early no longer costs you a fish.
3. Fixed the sailfish weight shown in the codex: it used to display a number smaller than the fish's own minimum, so heavier fish appeared lighter.
4. Fixed the catch bar sinking when you hold OK: holding OK used to move the bar down the track, the opposite of the on-screen hint "hold OK to lift, release to drop", so pressing felt like it did nothing. Now holding lifts the bar and releasing drops it, exactly as the hint says.
5. Fixed the catch bar getting stuck: after holding OK for a second or two on a big fish, releasing often left the bar pinned at the top until you tapped again. Now it rises the instant you press and drops the instant you let go, no matter how long you hold.
6. Fixed holding OK doing nothing right after the strike: the press that triggers the strike used to be swallowed, so keeping the button held did not raise the bar until you released and pressed again. Now you can hold straight through the strike.
7. OK responds faster: input no longer waits for a full "single click" to be recognised, and releasing is detected in about 0.01 s, so the lift no longer feels sluggish; quick double taps no longer swallow the release.
8. Smoother rendering: the background, text labels and the reel progress bar are only redrawn when they actually change. Previously the whole screen was repainted every frame, which was most visible while reeling and in the codex.
